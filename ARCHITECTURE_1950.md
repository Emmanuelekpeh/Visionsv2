# ARCHITECTURE_1950.md

**A rebuild plan for the `visions` film system as a 1950 hand-drawn Disney picture** —
Cinderella-era: ink & paint cels over painted boards, photographed on a multiplane
camera.

**Audience:** an engineer starting a fresh repo, with this one readable beside them.
**Status:** design document. Nothing here is implemented. Where a number or a decision
was *measured* in this repo it is marked `[measured]`; where it is a plausible reading
it is marked `[design]`; where it is unknown it says so.

> **On intellectual property.** Everything below is *craft* and *technique* — the
> production pipeline, the photographic method, the material properties of cel paint and
> gouache. Those are not owned by anyone. Reproducing a specific studio's characters,
> designs, or films is a separate question this document does not answer, and "it's for
> personal use" is not a legal analysis. Build the *method*; bring your own characters.

> **On training.** **This architecture requires no training at all, and that is a hard
> constraint, not a preference.** It is the owner's standing rule: *"takes forever just to
> produce mediocre results."* Every model named below is pretrained and used as shipped —
> `Anime2Sketch`, `BasicPBC`, `ControlNet`, `Depth Anything`. Everything else is
> deterministic code: the ink-and-paint pass, the multiplane composite, line boil,
> dry-brush, paper, and the grade. **There is no fine-tune, no LoRA, and no distillation
> anywhere on the critical path** — see §9.

## 0. The one-sentence doctrine

> You are not generating video. You are **drawing a small number of cels, painting a few
> boards, and deciding how a camera is going to move past them.**

This is a sharper version of the doctrine this repo already runs on:

> *"You are designing a sequence of discrete visual states, and deciding exactly when the
> world is allowed to change."* — [ANIMATION_SPEC.md](ANIMATION_SPEC.md) §0

The existing system took that sentence seriously **in the temporal dimension only**. It
built an exposure sheet, holds, per-layer clocks, sparse drawings, and a ban on
autoregressive chaining — and then rendered each drawing as a *full-frame opaque image*
generated by a video model, finished with camera shake, and graded to a shared palette.
That is a hand-drawn *timing* model wrapped around a *video generation* renderer. The
look is a film look, because the renderer is a film renderer.

The 1950 method differs from that in one structural way that changes everything:

```
THIS REPO                         1950 DISNEY
─────────                         ───────────
one opaque frame per drawing  →   a stack of transparent cels over a painted board
drawing = whole picture       →   drawing = ONE ELEMENT on ONE LEVEL
motion = the picture changes  →   motion = the camera moves past static art
the model invents the frame   →   the drawing decides the frame; the model fills it
```

**The single highest-value consequence:** a cel is *reusable and composable*. A character
drawn once can be held, re-exposed at a different position, and shot against three
different backgrounds. A painted board is shot for the whole sequence. Compare the
render budget of this repo — 8 shots × 12–16 diffusion frames, every frame a full
picture `[measured]` — against a method where the background is painted once per scene
and the character costs one drawing per pose.

### The five invariants, restated for cel

`ANIMATION_SPEC.md` §1 is still binding. Two need a new sentence.

| | as written | added clause for a hand-drawn pipeline |
|---|---|---|
| **INV-1** | no autoregressive drawing chains | **A character drawing is anchored to the character's model sheet, not to the previous drawing of that character.** This is the whole of on-model consistency, and it is the same rule. |
| **INV-2** | timing is authored, never implied | Unchanged. `on_twos` is a 1950 default, not a law. |
| **INV-3** | stillness is a first-class citizen | Unchanged, and *more* important: a Disney hold is a held cel, and a held cel costs zero drawings. |
| **INV-4** | no universal clock | Unchanged. The classic division is exactly this: background static, character on twos, effects on ones or held. |
| **INV-5** | the model draws states, not frames | **Restated: the model draws *one element on one level*, not a frame.** The frame is a composite. |

## 2. What is thrown away, and why

Be honest about this list. It is most of the render stack.

| Dropped | Why |
|---|---|
| **AnimateDiff v3 + DreamShaper 8 + motion LoRAs + Lightning 4-step** | The entire generation backbone. The measurement that killed it for this purpose: *"AnimateDiff's motion is global drift, not subject motion"* — top 5% of pixels hold 7.7% of temporal variance, near-uniform `[measured, HANDOFF §5.1]`. Drift is the *opposite* of what a cel pipeline wants. In a cel pipeline you do not want motion from the model at all: you want a **still drawing**, and the motion comes from the exposure sheet and the camera. This is not a small refactor. It is the reason to rebuild. |
| **RAFT / flow interpolation** | It was invented to make 12 frames into 51 by warping `[measured]`. It cannot invent a pose, and it smears line art — flow fields are estimated on photographs and a cel line is a discontinuity. Some modern inbetweeners (§5.5) may be worth re-testing, but the *architecture* must not need them. |
| **`handheld.py` — the procedural camera shake** | Built to make a synthetic clip read as "a held camera" `[measured, HANDOFF §6.1]`. A 1950 Disney picture is shot on a **locked-off multiplane rig or a locked tripod**. Shake is an anachronism and would read as noise over watercolor. The module is genuinely good work — it is simply the wrong century. It could be re-scoped to camera *drift* on long pans, but only as a deliberate exception. |
| **`grade_match.py` — the seam-matching grader** | It exists to make clips from *different models* look like one film `[measured]`, with 8 perceptual features, `SEAM_WEIGHTS`, `STYLE_MIN=1.5` / `STYLE_MAX=9.0` / `TECH_TOL=1.5` thresholds. In a cel pipeline you do not match two shots to each other — the **paint palette is shared by construction**. A scene's boards and cels come off the same chart. Keep the *idea* of a measurable palette; delete the seam machinery. |
| **The multi-model intercutting (`model_select.py`, `variety` objective)** | This repo spent real effort treating model choice as a creative variable and measured that *"the model-intercutting claim is NOT established"* `[measured, HANDOFF §9.9]`. A 1950 picture has **one** drawing style because one studio drew it. Deliberate style variation is the opposite of the goal. |
| **`effects.py` families — spatial / aberration / channel-separation** | A registered effect catalog across tonal/spatial/temporal/material/structural `[measured, effects.py FAMILY_ORDER]`. Most of it is 2020s video-art. What survives is a much smaller set: **dry-brush, airbrush, ripple glass, and a paper/grain texture.** §5.8. |
| **256px anything, fp16-on-CPU, the whole diffusion-time trap list** | Kept only as warnings — the traps in §11. Most will no longer apply because the models change, and *that is itself a risk*: new stack, new traps. Re-measure, do not assume the old list transfers. |

## 3. The target architecture

Six stages. Each names its input, its output, and whether the honest answer is **ML**,
**deterministic**, or **authored by a person**.

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  1. STORY        logline, beat sheet, sequence breakdown              [LLM]   │
│  2. CAST         model sheets: turnaround, expressions, PAINT CHART   [ML+auth]│
│  3. LAYOUT       field size, peg, plane assignment, camera move       [authored]│
│  4. ANIMATION    exposure sheet: keys, breakdowns, holds, levels      [authored]│
│  5. DRAW         pencils → line → PAINT → cel (RGBA)  ← the hard part [ML]    │
│  6. CAMERA       multiplane composite + exposure → frame sequence     [determ.]│
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                   ↓
                       boards painted (stage 3b) + camera (stage 6) → film
```

Note stages 5 and 6 are **separated**, and that separation is the architecture:

```
   BOARD PAINT ──┐
                 ├──► CAMERA (multiplane) ──► FRAME ──► EDIT ──► FILM
   CEL STACK ────┘
```

The frame is *assembled*, never *generated*. Nothing that produces a frame is stochastic.
Every stochastic thing in the system happens **upstream**, where it is cheap, inspectable,
and re-runnable without re-shooting the film.

### 3.0 Why the multiplane camera is the architectural keystone, not a feature

This is the part that is easy to miss. The multiplane camera is what makes a cel picture
feel like it has depth, and it is **the reason the 1950 look is even reachable by a
computational system**. It converts "how does the picture change" from a generation
problem into a **geometry** problem:

```
for each frame t:
    for each plane p in back-to-front order:
        transform = camera_transform(t, p.depth)        # pure math
        composite(transform(plane_p.content), over)     # pure compositing
```

Nothing here is learned, nothing is stochastic, and **the camera move is authored in
stage 3, before a single pixel is drawn**. A truck-in reveals new background because the
board is *larger than the frame* and the camera window slides across it. That is the
whole trick, and it is ~200 lines of affine math.

Consequence for the rebuild: **the parallax compositor is the first thing to build and the
last thing to break.** It can be developed and verified against flat colour rectangles
before any model exists. Do that — it de-risks the entire project, because it proves the
frame-assembly contract that everything else must satisfy.

## 4. Data model

Carry `film_store.py`'s discipline (stable ids, immutable takes, derived staleness) into a
production schema that has cast and scene as first-class things. Sketch, not gospel —
but the *shapes* are load-bearing.

```
productions/<slug>/
  project.json                 schema, logline, beats, cast[], scenes[], timeline[], settings
  cast/<char>.json             model sheet + PAINT CHART (the on-model anchor)
  scenes/<scene>.json          location, boards, palette, multiplane plan
  shots/<shot>.json            layout + camera + sheet summary
  sheets/<shot>.json           the ExposureSheet, serialised
  drawings/<drawing>.json      provenance: source, anchor, redraw_from, paint chart rev
  cels/<shot>/<drawing>.png    RGBA, transparent, the painted cel
  boards/<scene>/<plane>.png   RGB, larger than frame, the painted board
  planes/<shot>/<plane>.json   depth, parallax, content ref
  takes/<shot>/<take>/         IMMUTABLE: take.json + frames/  (as it works today)
  renders/<name>/              an export
```

### 4.1 Cast and the PAINT CHART — the piece this repo has no equivalent of

The single most important new object. A 1950 studio did not "prompt for a consistent
character"; it had a **model sheet** (the drawings) and a **paint chart** (the palette).

```jsonc
// cast/ella.json
{
  "id": "ella",
  "role": "protagonist",
  "model_sheet": {
    "turnaround": ["front", "three-quarter", "profile", "back"],
    "expressions": ["neutral", "wonder", "fear", "resolve"],
    "height_heads": 6.5,              // head-heights tall; a 1950 heroine reads ~6-7
    "silhouette_line": "clean, thin, continuous; no interior detail below 3px"
  },
  "paint_chart": {                     // THE reference. Flat, indexed, closed set.
    "hair":       {"hex": "#4A2E1E", "ink": "#2B1A10"},
    "skin":       {"hex": "#F0C9A8", "ink": "#6B4436", "blush": "#D98C7A"},
    "dress":      {"hex": "#7FA8C9", "ink": "#2E4A63", "shadow": "#5C82A4"},
    "eye":        {"hex": "#3E5A44", "ink": "#1C2A20"},
    "line":       {"hex": "#241A14", "weight_px": [2, 4], "taper": true}
  },
  "anchor_drawing": "ella_model_front"   // the AUTHORED root every other drawing ANCHORs to
}
```

Two rules, and they are the entire consistency strategy:

1. **`paint_chart` is a closed set.** No colour reaches a cel that is not on the chart.
   This is enforceable mechanically and is the reason a cel pipeline is *more* consistent
   than a diffusion one — you cannot drift to a different character if you are not
   choosing colours, you are *indexing* them.
2. **Every drawing of a character is `ANCHORED` to `anchor_drawing`.** Not to the previous
   drawing. `animation_spec.validate()` already raises `AutoregressiveChainError` if you
   try to anchor to an anchor `[measured, animation_spec.py:755-775]`. The existing
   invariant is the on-model invariant.

### 4.2 Plane — the object this repo does not have at all

```jsonc
// planes/<shot>/<plane>.json
{
  "name": "bg_far",
  "depth": 8.0,            // arbitrary units; parallax derives from it
  "parallax": 0.15,        // camera travel multiplier. 0 = infinity, 1 = at the plane of action
  "content": {"kind": "board", "ref": "scenes/forest/board_far"},
  "origin": [0, 0],        // board corner in field coordinates; boards are BIGGER than the frame
  "over": ["bg_mid"],      // explicit compositing order, back to front
  "softness": 0.0          // 0 at plane of action; >0 for a slight far-plane defocus
}
```

`parallax` is the whole of the depth illusion. Field-tested values `[design]`:

| plane | depth | parallax | content | typical use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bg_far` | 8.0 | 0.10–0.20 | painted board, sky/treeline | never moves with the character |
| `bg_mid` | 4.0 | 0.35–0.45 | painted board, nearer masses | the main parallax reveal |
| `bg_near` | 2.0 | 0.65–0.75 | painted board or a held cel | foreground foliage the camera passes |
| `action` | 0.0 | **1.00** | character cels | the plane of action; defines "correct" speed |
| `over` | −1.0 | 1.10–1.25 | foreground cel (branch, pillar) | occludes; sells the depth |

**A single-plane shot is legal and often correct.** The failure mode of this technique is
parallax on everything; a 1950 picture uses the multiplane where it earns its keep.

### 4.3 Extending the exposure sheet for levels

`Layer` already carries `name`, `track`, `clock`, `loop`, `static`
`[measured, animation_spec.py:564]`. Two additions, both additive:

```python
# Level = Layer + the two things a camera department needs
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Level:
    layer: Layer                    # reuse, do not subclass: the sheet stays authoritative
    plane: str                      # -> planes/<shot>/<plane>.json
    element: Optional[str] = None   # which cast member, for the paint chart lookup
    origin: Tuple[float, float] = (0.0, 0.0)   # per-frame offset drives position changes
```

`ExposureSheet.frame_state(frame)` already returns `{layer_name: drawing_id}` for a frame
`[measured, animation_spec.py:654]`. Extend that dict's values to
`(drawing_id, plane, origin)` and the compositor has everything it needs. Properties no
longer hold:

- A layer no longer maps 1:1 to a full-frame image.
- Two layers can share a plane; one layer can change plane *within* a shot (a character
  walking from background to foreground). That is `origin` plus a plane change, and it is
  a real animation decision, so it belongs in the sheet.

### 4.4 The generation hash, extended

This repo's best structural result is that **staleness is derived, never stored**, by
hashing the real dependencies: `generation_inputs` (prompt, camera, scale, subject, seed,
model, frames) and `rebuild_hash` (hold, shake, palette, scene palettes, in-point)
`[measured, HANDOFF §0.1]`. Keep it exactly, and keep the two rules that came out of the
expensive bug — append new keys to a legacy-tolerance list, and never write a new default
into an existing project `[measured, HANDOFF §0.4 trap 9]`.

New dependencies:

| hash | gains |
|---|---|
| `generation_inputs` | `paint_chart_rev`, `anchor_drawing`, `line_model`, `redraw_model` |
| `rebuild_hash` | `plane_depths`, `camera_move`, `board_rev`, `paper_texture_rev`, `ink_boil_seed` |

`paint_chart_rev` is the important one: **edit a colour on the chart and every cel using
it becomes stale** — which is correct, and is the cel-pipeline equivalent of "changed the
prompt". Note that this makes the chart a *slow* object to change. That is a feature: it
should be expensive to restyle a character after 200 drawings.

## 5. The stages in detail

### 5.1 STORY — `[LLM]`, keep it

Port `story.py` essentially whole. The additions are period-shaped, not structural:

- **Sequence, not just shot list.** A 1950 picture is organised as sequences, and the
  multiplane reveal is a *sequence-level* decision. Add a `sequence` field alongside
  `beat`, and let `story_shape` report `sequences × beats`.
- **Business.** The thing that makes a Disney shot read as character rather than as a
  landscape is *business* — what the figure is doing with their hands. Add a required
  `business` field per shot ("she sets the tray down and does not look at it"). The
  existing `link_overlap` mechanism is the right model for validating that the business
  carries through the beats.
- Keep `BEATS` = establish/develop/turn/resolve, the re-roll loop, `prompt_leaks`, and the
  farm interleaving with `FARM_QUOTA` `[measured, HANDOFF §11.3]`.

### 5.2 CAST — `[ML + authored]`, new

For each principal:
1. Author or generate a **turnaround** (4 views) and an **expression set**.
2. **Derive the paint chart from the turnaround, not from taste.** Cluster the colours in
   the turnaround and propose a chart; a human flattens it to the final ~6–10 entries.
   This is the one place in the pipeline where a human should be in the loop *by
   construction*, because every cel downstream indexes into this chart.
3. Freeze the chart. Bump `paint_chart_rev` to invalidate.

### 5.3 LAYOUT — `[authored]`, new

Per shot: field size, peg registration, plane assignment, camera move, and duration.

**Field size and framing.** A 1950 picture works in fields, not in pixels, and the field
system is a real spec rather than a vibe `[researched]`:

- A **field measures the shape of the screen**, not a length. `1 field` is ~1in × 0.75in;
  **`12 field` is 12in wide by ~8.85in (4:3)** and is the common default; **`16 field` is the
  largest commonly used**.
- **The centre is not the middle of the paper.** The drawing centre sits **5.75 inches
  directly above the centre of the round peg hole**, marked with the crossed "C" —
  "Academy centre." For larger 4:3 fields the centre *cannot* stay the same, because the peg
  bar would intrude into the artwork.
- A **15-field widescreen framing can keep the standard 12-field centre.**

That last point is directly load-bearing for §4.2: **fields and the peg define where the
camera window may sit, and therefore how much board must exist around the frame.** Store the
field size, the peg/centre convention, and the camera window all in **field units**, and
convert to pixels exactly once, in the compositor. Do not scatter pixel math through the
stages — and compute the required board size (§5.7) from these same units.

**Camera move**, as data, authored before anything is drawn:

```jsonc
"camera": {
  "move": "truck-in",              // locked | truck-in/out | pan-* | tilt-* | crane-* | rack
  "from": [0.50, 0.50, 1.00],      // field x, field y, zoom
  "to":   [0.50, 0.50, 0.62],
  "ease": "smooth",                // reuse animation_spec.Easing
  "over_frames": 96
}
```

Reuse `Easing` — it already has `OVERSHOOT` and `ANTICIPATE` which leave `[0,1]`
`[measured, animation_spec.py:187-192]`, and a camera move that overshoots and settles is
a real 1950 device.

**The one rule to carry from the existing director:** *default to no camera move.* This
repo measured that AnimateDiff already drifts and forcing a move on every shot is
exhausting `[measured, HANDOFF §6]`. The same fatigue applies here, for a different
reason: a multiplane move costs board area and reveals seams, so spend it.

### 5.4 ANIMATION — `[authored]`, the exposure sheet

This is `animation_spec.py` doing what it was built for. A shot is:

```
keys and breakdowns (KeyPose, role=BREAKDOWN)
   → MotionPlan.states()      sparse: ~7-14 states, not 24/sec   [measured, plan.md §10]
   → ExposureTrack.on_twos()  a 1950 default
   → Layer per level, clock per level
   → ExposureSheet.validate() INV-1..INV-5
```

Three things to add:

- **`on_twos` as the house default.** The engine already has the constructor
  `[measured, ANIMATION_SPEC.md §2]`. Changing a *default* is not changing the doctrine:
  authored timing is still authored, twos is still just the common case.
- **Mouth charts.** Dialogue and even silent mouth shapes are a discrete track, exactly
  like a drawing track — a `Layer` on its own clock indexed by phoneme. `Layer.clock =
  Fraction(1,2)` is the standard. This is a genuinely good fit for the existing model and
  should be a first-class level type, not an afterthought.
- **Effects as levels.** Water, fire, smoke, and sparkle were separate departments and
  were *cycled* — a fixed set of drawings reused in a loop. `LoopPolicy.REPEAT` already
  exists `[measured, animation_spec.py:388]`. This is the period-correct use of it.
- **Do not let a speed measurement overrule the eye on the number of drawings.** The
  single most expensive lesson in this repo's history, and the one most likely to be
  re-learned here. See §8.8.

### 5.5 DRAW — `[ML]`, **this is the hard part and the project's real risk**

The honest framing: **the model does not draw the film.** It draws *individual elements on
levels*, at the lowest possible resolution of decision, and a deterministic pass imposes
the ink-and-paint look. Split into four sub-stages, ordered by how much they matter.

```
  (a) PENCIL      pose or rough for one element      ← ML, low fidelity needed
        ↓
  (b) LINE        clean ink line, transparent bg     ← ML + deterministic cleanup  ← CRITICAL
        ↓
  (c) PAINT       flat fill from the chart, cel RGBA ← DETERMINISTIC (flood fill + chart)
        ↓
  (d) CEL         the finisher: paper, dry-brush, ink weight
```

**(b) is the whole ballgame.** A cel is a *line drawing with flat fills*. Diffusion models
produce gradients, texture, and soft edges; a cel is the opposite — a hard-edged, few-
colour, high-contrast graphic. The existing repo has already measured what happens when
you ask a small diffusion model for a shape change: with `tiny-sd` calibrated directly on
a red ball, *"it renders a red ball and ignores every modifier. Shape and position control
do not exist in this model"* `[measured, ANIMATION_SPEC.md §6.2]`. The same failure applies
to asking a general diffusion model for "the same character, arm 20° further forward".
**Never build the pipeline on that request.** Build it on:

| sub-stage | approach | why |
|---|---|---|
| (a) pencil | ControlNet (lineart/openpose) or a LoRA'd SD-family model, prompted for a *rough*, at whatever resolution, then discarded | low fidelity needed; this output is never seen |
| (b) line | **`Anime2Sketch`** or **Simo-Serra et al. 2016** (rough-sketch cleanup), then a deterministic pass: threshold, skeletonize, re-weight | the *only* stage where a cel's defining quality is set — and both of these *re-synthesize* a line rather than cleaning up yours, see below |
| (b′) redraw (inbetweens) | **`AnimeInbet`** (line-level) or **`ToonCrafter`**/`ToonComposer` (generative) are worth measuring; the architecture must not *need* them | see below — none of them hold vector-grade edges |
| (c) paint | **region segmentation of closed line art → flat fill per region using the chart** — the digital ink & paint department. `BasicPBC` (inclusion matching, CVPR 2024) and `Style2Paints` are the real methods; the CVPR 2021 split-filling paper states the flat-fill model | deterministic, fast, and *exactly* the consistency mechanism |
| (d) cel finish | ink weight variation, dry-brush edges, paper grain, and **line boil** | see §5.6 |

**The named tooling, with the honest verdict on each.** These are real and current; the
survey to read is *Generative AI for Cel-Animation: A Survey* (arXiv 2501.06250, ICCV 2025
AISTORY workshop), whose own framing is that the cel pipeline is "storyboarding, layout
design, keyframe animation, inbetweening, and colorization" — note that it does **not**
list cleanup, ink, paint or the camera, which is where this project's difficulty lives.

| stage | tool | honest verdict |
|---|---|---|
| line | `Anime2Sketch`; Simo-Serra *Learning to Simplify* (SIGGRAPH 2016) | The practical default — ToonCrafter itself uses `Anime2Sketch` to make its training sketches. But it is trained on anime/illustration and **re-synthesizes a new line drawing**; it will normalise line weight in ways a Disney cleanup artist would not, and it cannot reproduce *your* animator's hand. There is no ML substitute for what cleanup actually did, which was **enforce the model sheet**, not thin lines. |
| flat paint | `BasicPBC` / inclusion matching (CVPR 2024); `Style2Paints`; split filling (CVPR 2021) | **The best-served stage, and the match is structural rather than approximate** — Disney's stage really is "close the regions, then fill flat." Two hard caveats: every one of these **requires closed line art** (a broken line is a flood leak), and they inherit whatever the line stage got wrong. If the line is diffusion-shaped, the fills are diffusion-shaped. |
| inbetweening | `AnimeInterp`, `EISAI`, `AnimeInbet`, `JoSTC`, `ToonCrafter`, `ToonComposer`, `RIFE`, `FILM` | **None preserves vector-grade line fidelity.** ToonCrafter's own paper is the admission: correspondence methods "drifting" and "dissolving" hands, and generative methods hitting latent compression where "high-contrast regions, the fine structural outline, and the lack of motion blur in cartoon animations" make degradation *more* visible than in live action — bad enough that they built a bespoke dual-reference detail-injecting decoder to stop outlines being destroyed. And **every one of them interpolates uniformly in time**, which is precisely what a timing director exists to prevent. |
| character consistency | `IP-Adapter`, `InstantID`, ControlNet reference-only | **Not needed, and deliberately not used.** These preserve **facial identity for photographic subjects**; Disney consistency is **line-and-volume consistency against a model sheet**, holding for hands, ears, drapery folds, and the shape of a nose in three-quarter profile. IP-Adapter's authors concede the CLIP embedding gives only coarse alignment; InstantID's concede "highly coupled facial attributes." **No published method takes a model sheet as a hard constraint** — so this architecture does not ask one to. Consistency comes from the **paint chart plus the model-sheet anchor**, both deterministic (§4.1). That is also why it needs no training. |
| boards | **pretrained** style model, used as shipped — no fine-tune. If a ready-made gouache/watercolor checkpoint does not do it, fall back to a **procedural board** (§9) | Mature as a *use* of someone else's model, and the *least* load-bearing stage. Diffusion puts texture everywhere, which fights flat cels and makes characters read as pasted on. And training a style LoRA here would mean training on Disney's copyrighted frames — which §9 rules out on two grounds. |
| multiplane | **this one is a genuine architectural match** | The multiplane *is already* a depth-ordered layer stack with per-layer motion — so "estimate depth, cut into planes, animate by depth" reproduces the mechanism rather than approximating it. See §5.7 for what it does **not** reproduce. |

**On line boil.** This is the one place where a naive engineer will "fix" the aesthetic
into something wrong. Hand-traced ink lines wobble slightly drawing to drawing. It reads as
alive. A deterministic vectorization pass makes every line perfectly still, which reads as
dead and instantly dates the image as digital.

The research settled the cadence, and it is the part that is easy to get wrong: **boil is
stable *within* a held cel and changes *between* drawings.** The only purpose-built tool
found (`scofano/comfy-line-boil`, a ComfyUI node) makes this explicit with a `hold`
parameter — "reuse the same boil pattern for consecutive frames," default 2 — plus a fixed
seed for determinism `[researched, §5j]`. Nobody tries to *learn* boil, because every ML
interpolator's native behaviour is to remove it. So: an explicit seeded procedural pass,
`hold` tied to the exposure sheet's own hold length, and `ink_boil_seed` in `rebuild_hash`
so a re-boil is a re-shoot.

**On redraws vs. drawing every inbetween.** `DrawingSource.REDRAWN` exists and is
validated so that a redraw may never derive from another redraw
`[measured, tests/test_redraw_spec.py]`. Keep that exactly. Then: the *safest* production
choice is to author breakdowns and let `inbetweens=n` be zero (a cut) or a genuine
generated drawing. A learned inbetweener that preserves line topology would be a large
win — it would make twos into ones cheaply — but line art is exactly where optical-flow
inbetweeners fail, and the existing repo correctly removed its RAFT-based `redrawer.py`
because *"warping two keys smears unrelated pixels and cannot invent a pose that appears
in neither key"* `[measured, tests/test_redraw_spec.py:1-14]`. **Treat inbetweening as an
optimisation to be measured, never as a dependency.**

**Two structural facts about the paint stage that constrain the data model:**

- **Ink and paint are on opposite sides of the cel.** The line is **inked on the front**;
  the paint is applied **opaque on the reverse** `[researched, §1.7]`. So the line is a
  *separate occluding layer*, not a colour boundary inside an image. That is what makes the
  "line as mask, paint as index" split in this section the physically correct one rather
  than a computational convenience — and it is the exact thing diffusion gets wrong, since
  it paints *through* lines and bleeds colour across contours.
- **A real cel stack was capped at about five levels.** *"Animation seldom exceeds five
  levels of acetate cels; any more and they will seriously darken the lower levels"*
  `[researched, §1.7]`. This is the direct technical reason paint **had to be opaque**: a
  translucent fill on a lower cel would be muddied by everything above it. Two
  consequences here — a hard cap is period-correct and should be *enforced* by the
  compositor rather than discovered, and **the cap is a feature**: it is why a background
  could not simply be another cel and had to be a separate painted board underneath.

### 5.6 The look — `[deterministic]`, and where the period actually lives

The period is not in the model. It is in these, all of which are cheap, inspectable, and
**require no training** (§9):

| device | implementation | note |
|---|---|---|
| **flat paint** | indexed fill from the paint chart; hard edges, no gradient inside a region | the single strongest signal. Forced by the medium, not chosen: paint was opaque and the stack was capped near five levels, so a translucent fill would be muddied by everything above it. |
| **ink line** | a separate *occluding* layer — hard-edged, slightly broken on the interior | **physically correct, not a stylistic choice**: the line was inked on the front of the cel and the paint applied to the *reverse*, so the line genuinely sits in front of the fill. Do not model it as a colour boundary inside an image. |
| **line boils *within* a held cel, not within a frame** | seeded sub-pixel wobble, re-rolled **per drawing**, with the pattern held constant across that drawing's exposure | the cadence is the part that is easy to get wrong — see §5.5 |
| **shadow as a flat shape** | a second, darker *indexed* chart colour as a shape — **not** a lighting calculation | a cel has no internal shading. Every bit of "modelling" on a character has to be *drawn* as an extra flat shape. This is the most commonly botched detail. |
| **boards, not backgrounds** | a separate painted board *below* the whole cel stack, painted in a gouache/watercolor idiom with visible paper texture | `[researched, inferred]` The board is the only element that carries painted light and atmosphere. **Do not over-claim the "characters flat / boards lit" contrast as period doctrine** — the research could not source it as a quoted statement, and it is a strong inference from the medium rather than a documented rule. It is a good working principle; state it as one. |
| **restricted palette** | a closed chart per character and per scene | the structural reason the medium is consistent — a flat, hand-mixed, pre-judged value per region |
| **line colour is not always black** | allow per-element line colour, not a global ink colour | `[researched]` Real conventions existed: **gray outlines** on characters, **white and blue toners for snow and water**, and the term of art **"self-colored lines."** Put `line` in the paint chart per element rather than hard-coding black. |
| **paper / cel texture** | a shared subtle grain over the composite, plus a hair of gate weave | |
| **Technicolor-ish response** | a restrained curve, a slightly warm highlight, slight halation | *restrained.* The real process was **three-strip Technicolor (Process 4)** with **IB dye-transfer** printing — celebrated for saturation, which is the easy thing to overdo. Note the process's real artifacts if you are matching a scan: the three strips have **different contrast** and **shrink at different rates**, which is why restorations align on the **green** record. |

**Explicitly out of scope:** any effect that reads as 2020s video art. The existing
`effects.py` catalog spans tonal/spatial/temporal/material/structural families
`[measured, effects.py:101]` and most should not be ported. What ports: dry-brush,
airbrush, ripple glass, and grain/paper. What does not: channel separation, chromatic
aberration, displacement, halftone/dither as a *look*.

### 5.7 CAMERA — `[deterministic]`

```
for frame in sheet.total_frames:                # animation_spec already gives this
    canvas = boards_in_plane_order()            # back to front
    for plane in planes_back_to_front():
        content = cel_at(frame, plane) or board(plane)
        canvas.alpha_composite(parallax(content, camera_window(frame), plane.parallax))
    emit(canvas)
```

Properties to verify, and they are all cheap assertions:

- **The camera does not pan — it moves the artwork.** `[researched]` On a real rostrum and
  on the multiplane, "pans are made by moving the cels or backgrounds one step at a time;
  the camera does not pan, it only zooms in and out." That is a **gift** to a computational
  build: pan is a translation of the plane stack, zoom is a scale about the centre, and
  both are exact affine math with no resampling of the *camera* — only of the layer. Model
  it that way and the "camera move" and the "parallax" are the same operation, which is why
  §3.0 calls the compositor the keystone.
- **Five levels is a hard ceiling, and it exists for a physical reason.** The stack was
  capped near five cels because more "seriously darken the lower levels." Enforce the cap
  in the compositor rather than discovering it. It also explains why a background could not
  simply be one more cel: it had to be a separate board *underneath* everything.
- **Registration is real, so model it.** Cels carried peg holes and misalignment reads as
  jitter at speed. Acme pegs (two slots, one round hole) are the most common convention, and
  **Oxberry** rostrum cameras commonly had two peg bars (one top, one bottom) — the Master
  Series had four and sometimes a "floating peg bar." You do not need the hardware, but a
  `registration` field per plane is the right place to hold a deliberate, small
  misalignment — which is a *look* device, not a bug.
- **No fill pixels at any frame, for any shot.** This repo learned this the hard way: its
  camera-resample acceptance test originally checked for black border pixels and passed on
  three shots while failing on two, because a bicubic kernel reaches ~2px beyond its
  coordinate and sampled the fill `[measured, HANDOFF §6.1 trap]`. **The fix is a sentinel
  fill colour that nothing else in the pipeline can produce** (magenta), and the assertion
  is zero sentinel pixels in the delivered frames. Apply this rule to the multiplane
  compositor on day one.
- **The camera window never leaves the board.** Boards must be painted larger than the
  maximum camera excursion. Compute the required board size *from* the authored camera
  move and report it at layout time — so the background department knows how big to paint,
  before it paints.
- **Parallax is monotonic in depth** and the action plane is exactly 1.0.
- **Frame count equals `sheet.total_frames`** with no pad and no trim. The existing spec
  already demands this: `fit_to_frames()` hits the duration exactly and a residual mismatch
  raises rather than being papered over `[measured, ANIMATION_SPEC.md §4]`.

### 5.8 EDIT + AUDIO

- **Edit.** R1–R5 from `edit_planner.py` carry over. Add the period device that this repo
  has no concept of: **dissolves**. R5 currently says "hard cuts only" `[measured, HANDOFF
  §6]` — that was correct for the previous system and is *wrong* for 1950, where a dissolve
  is a standard transition. Add dissolves to the exposure model as an overlap of two
  sheets, not as a video crossfade.
- **Audio.** The existing gap, and it is *larger* here, not smaller: a 1950 picture is
  driven by a **music score and a click track**. Animation was timed to bars and beats,
  and the exposure sheet is where that lands. Build the sketch track first; it is the
  cheapest and most period-accurate step in the whole system.

  **But do not quantise to it.** The house position in this project is that the thing
  that makes music feel alive is *"making things slightly late, uneven, or improperly
  placed instead of quantizing everything"* — and traditional animation has exactly the
  same device. The beat grid is a **reference**, not a ruler: author the sheet *against*
  the grid so that the deviations are visible and deliberate, then let a hold run a
  half-beat long, let a snap land a frame early, let the resolve sit two frames after the
  downbeat. A pipeline that rounds every hold to the nearest beat will produce something
  mechanically correct and dead — the animation equivalent of a click-tracked session.

  Practically: store exposure in **frames** (as `Hold.frames` already does), store the
  beat grid as a separate object, and have the studio *show the offset* between a hold's
  end and the nearest beat. The deviation becomes a visible, tunable creative value
  instead of a rounding error. `Easing.ANTICIPATE` and `Easing.OVERSHOOT` are the
  existing expression of the same instinct, one level down.

## 6. Where AI helps and where it will hurt you

Blunt, because this repo's most valuable cultural trait is refusing to let a metric
overrule an eye.

| Stage | AI verdict |
|---|---|
| Story, beats, business, dialogue | **Yes, keep.** LLM text is the strongest-performing part of the existing system `[measured]`. |
| Model sheet *drafts*, expression *ideas* | **Yes**, as drafts a person flattens. |
| Board painting (gouache/watercolor idiom) | **Yes, with a style LoRA.** The board is the one component where diffusion's texture is *wanted*. |
| Pencil / rough pose | **Yes, low fidelity needed**, discard after. |
| **Line and on-model consistency** | **This is the risk.** Do not ask a diffusion model to hold a character on model across 200 drawings. Anchor to the model sheet and impose consistency in the *paint* stage, where it is deterministic. |
| **Inbetweening** | **Measure, do not assume.** Line topology is where flow-based methods fail. |
| **Flat cel paint** | **No.** Deterministic segmentation + indexed fill is faster, exact, and debuggable. A model here would be a downgrade. |
| **Camera / multiplane** | **No.** Geometry. |
| Line boil, dry brush, paper | **No.** Seeded procedural. Must be *reproducible*, which a model is not. |
| Grade / Technicolor response | **No.** A curve, in a file, reviewable. |

**The general principle to carry forward:** *use the model where the output is inspected
and discarded; use deterministic code where the output is a contract.* Every stochastic
element belongs upstream of the cel.

## 7. The studio, re-cut for a camera department

The three-verb doctrine survives; the views change, because the *work* changed. The old
middle verb was "shoot" = run a render. In a cel pipeline, shooting is the cheap last
step, and the expensive work has moved earlier.

| view | what it is | one primary action |
|---|---|---|
| **write** | the story, then the sequence and shot list | write the shot list |
| **cast** | model sheets and **paint charts**. Small, rarely touched, gates everything | freeze the chart |
| **board** | per scene: the painted boards and the plane depths | paint the boards |
| **animate** | the exposure sheet, the shoot's 95%. Dope sheet on screen, level by level | draw the next pose |
| **shoot** | the multiplane composite. Fast — this is where "shoot" stops being the expensive verb | shoot it |
| **cut** | assemble, dissolve, and export | export the film |

The design rules from `ui/studio.py`'s header stay binding, and three of them now do
new work `[measured, START_HERE.md]`:

- **One primary action per view** — the six above are an *increase* from three and that is a
  warning sign. Consider merging `cast`+`board` into one "art" view, since both are
  "make the things the animation is drawn on". Six views is already a lot.
- **State as a word, not a colour.** A cel is either drawn, inked, painted, or not. Four
  words.
- **The price before the button.** The right price to show in `animate` is **drawings**, not
  seconds — `ExposureSheet.cost_report()` already produces exactly that
  `[measured, animation_spec.py:712]`. Show `generation_calls`, `redraw_calls`, and
  `held_or_reused_frames`. A hold costing zero drawings should be visible on the button.

The self-refreshing fragment and the 2-second poll against a 1.5s `state.json` write are
both still correct `[measured, START_HERE.md]`. Keep them.

### 8.2 `film_store.py` — the production book

`SCHEMA = 2`. Layout and the exact field lists are in §4. Notes that matter for a port:

- **Staleness is derived, never stored** `[measured, film_store.py:806-861]`. The state
  machine is exactly `unrendered | stale | restale | rendered | approved`, and
  `needs_generation(state)` is true only for `unrendered`/`stale`. Approval does **not**
  short-circuit the rebuild check — so drift is reported *through* an approval rather than
  masked by it. Keep that.
- **The generation hash has exactly seven keys**: `prompt`, `scale`, `camera`, `subject`,
  `seed`, `model`, `frames` → `sha1(json.dumps(inputs, sort_keys=True))[:12]`
  `[measured, film_store.py:510-549]`. `seed` is frozen onto the shot by `ensure_seed`
  (`base + 101*n`) so a reorder cannot re-roll it — that is the fix for the
  order-addressed bug.
- **`rebuild_hash`** covers `finish_inputs` (`seconds, fps, shake_scale, palette,
  technical_baseline`), `pixel_deps`, `in_s`, and **every scene's palette**
  `[measured, film_store.py:666-699]`. `in_s` is **passed in** by one shared
  `in_points(project)` walk, never re-walked per shot — walking it per shot was the
  self-referential bug that made re-finish state append-only and never self-healing
  `[measured, HANDOFF §0.2 trap 3]`.
- **Legacy tolerance:** `ADDED_GEN_KEYS = ("frames",)`. A stored hash counts as current if
  it matches the current inputs *or* the current inputs minus those keys
  `[measured, film_store.py:552-588]`.
- **A take is immutable**: `takes/<shot>/<take>/take.json` + `frames/f%03d.png`;
  `next_take_name` returns the lowest free `tNN`, never an index; `take` is deliberately
  not a pixel input.
- `remove_shot(..., delete_files=False)` is the default and **keeps takes on disk** — this
  is what makes `draft.py` re-adoption non-destructive of finished renders.
- Watch out: `NON_PIXEL_FIELDS` excludes `scene`, `subject` and `energy` from hashing
  `[measured, film_store.py:619-622]` — even though the director consumes `subject` and
  `energy`. In a rebuild where a character's *identity* is `subject`-like, that decision
  needs re-taking (§4.4).

### 8.5 `job_runner.py` — the supervision contract, and three things in it that are easy to lose

A rebuild will want to rewrite this, and three of its mechanisms are the difference
between a UI that lies and one that does not.

**The file protocol.** One job = one directory `output/jobs/<id>/` containing `state.json`,
`log.txt`, `supervisor.txt`, and a `stop` flag. `output/jobs/.current` holds the running
job id. The UI **never spawns anything and never blocks** — it only reads `state.json` and
tails `log.txt` `[measured, ui/studio.py]`. `state.json` is written by tmp-then-replace
with **up to four attempts and a direct-write fallback, and it never raises**
`[measured, job_runner.py:310-355]`. That fallback is not defensive padding: the Windows
failure it exists for is that the 2-second UI poll holds a handle on `state.json`, so
`tmp.replace()` fails with a sharing violation and the exception escaped the supervisor —
**killing the supervisor and orphaning a running render.** Two drafts were lost to it, and
the 1.5-second heartbeat made it frequent by taking writes from once per 30–60 s to once
per 1.5 s `[measured, job_runner.py:311-329]`. Port this exactly; it *will* recur.

**Three mechanisms, in order of how much they cost to lose:**

1. **The liveness probe fails toward ALIVE.** `_alive(pid)` reports alive on *any* failure
   of the probe `[measured, job_runner.py:358-395]` — deliberately, so a false "dead" can
   never start a second render on a machine that is already busy. It uses
   `OpenProcess(PROCESS_QUERY_LIMITED_INFORMATION)` + `GetExitCodeProcess`, and it
   explicitly rejects two tempting alternatives: `tasklist /FI "PID eq N"` (did not list
   processes here) and **`os.kill(pid, 0)` on Windows, where CPython maps it to
   `TerminateProcess` — it would have killed the render it was asking about**
   `[measured, job_runner.py:366-390]`. If you write your own probe, know that last one.
2. **The supervisor's pid is not the child's pid.** `state["pid"]` is the *supervisor*,
   `state["child_pid"]` is the render. Recording the child's pid as the job pid released
   the lock during the finish window `[measured, job_runner.py:617-631]`. The supervisor
   also kills its child in a `finally:` on unexpected unwind, and there is a test asserting
   that source shape `[measured, recon_test/test_job_runner_io.py:73-81]`.
3. **The lock is a plain write and the check-then-lock is racy.** `start()` checks
   `active_job()` then writes the lock — not atomic, no `O_EXCL` `[measured,
   job_runner.py:451-471]`. It survives because the UI is the only starter and it is
   single-threaded. If a rebuild ever starts jobs from anywhere else, make this atomic; the
   self-clearing stale lock (`active_job` unlinks a lock whose state is gone or whose job
   is terminal) is otherwise correct and should be kept.

**Progress is parsed from the child's own prints, permissively.** Prefixes like
`=== GENERATING ===`, `2/6 zoom-in 468s -> take`, `[farm 1/3] qwen2.5:3b ...`. The
contract is explicit: an unknown line changes nothing, and a parser exception is swallowed
per line `[measured, job_runner.py:62-64, 648-652]`. That is the right trade — a render
must not die because a log line was reworded.

**And the studio's own version of the same idea, worth stealing wholesale:**
`progress_facts(job)` returns the **arithmetic** and `progress_view` renders it
`[measured, ui/studio.py:237-356]`. Facts is a pure function of the state dict, tested
directly with no browser and no fixtures on disk
`[measured, recon_test/test_studio.py:221-280]`. Two details inside it are right for any
rebuild: the estimator **never reads complete while running**
(`min(0.99, elapsed/est)`), and elapsed is reported **only while the state is RUNNING** —
*"reporting 'running 40 min' for something that stopped is exactly the kind of stale claim
this UI is meant to avoid"* `[measured, ui/studio.py:250-254]`. The reason two bars exist
rather than one is also worth keeping: with one bar, a 9–14 minute step is either invisible
between prints or sits at 0% for a quarter of an hour, which *"reads as a hang."*

### 8.6 Two things the audit found that change the port plan

**There are two project stores, and only one of them is the model.** `film_store.py` →
`productions/<slug>/` is current: per-shot files, timeline order as a list of ids,
immutable takes, no frozen plan. `film_project.py` → `workspace/<film>.json` is the older
workbench: a single JSON, and shot ids that are **positional integers**
`[measured, film_project.py:146-153]` — the exact position-addressed model `film_store.py`
was written to remove. `draft.py` writes only the store; the legacy one survives behind
`make_film.py --from-project`. **Port `film_store` and delete the other one.** Carrying
both forward would reintroduce the reorder bug four ways.

**`make_film.py` has no super-resolution stage at all.** The only resolution/motion step
in the current renderer is RAFT ×2 (16 raw → 31 frames; `factor=2` yields `2N-1`)
`[measured, make_film.py:402-433]`. SR exists solely in the legacy
`production_system.Assembler` path, and it loads `sr_resnet.SRResNet`, *not* the
`sr_upscaler.SRUpscaler` sitting next to it `[measured, production_system.py:572-609]`.
So there is no SR decision to inherit — a rebuild decides it fresh, and for a cel pipeline
the honest answer is that **you should not need SR at all**: a cel is drawn at delivery
resolution, and upscaling line art is how you get the soft-edge problem back. Draw at
final size.

**`production_system.py` is legacy and should mostly be dropped.** The audit confirms its
latent-flow branch is unreachable (`USE_LATENT_FLOW = False`) and contains an **undefined
global** — `MIN_EFFECTIVE_IMG2IMG_STEPS` is referenced twice and defined nowhere
`[measured, production_system.py:831,963]`. What is worth mining from it is
`build_exposure_sheet` (the legacy→sheet bridge) and the exact `SpecViolation` on a
frame-count mismatch `[measured, production_system.py:1266-1272]`.

### 8.7 Three behavioural details that are easy to get wrong

- **`apply_handheld` requires frames already at the *delivered* length**
  `[measured, handheld.py:559-588]`. In the current pipeline the order is stretch → shake,
  and the stretch uses *the same index map as `render()`* so `render`'s own resample is the
  identity. That coupling is fragile. In a rebuild the multiplane camera consumes the
  sheet directly, so the stretch step **disappears** — but if you keep any resample
  anywhere, keep this rule.
- **`effects.Effect.run` filters overrides on `is not None`, never truthiness**
  `[measured, effects.py:71-83]`. A truthiness check silently dropped `softness=0.0` and
  `ordered=False` — i.e. it made two parameters unsettable to their most meaningful
  values. Keep the rule wherever you keep a parameter dict.
- **`ExposureSheet.fit_to_frames()` mutates `self` and returns `self`**
  `[measured, animation_spec.py:661-672]`, and it rounds *per layer*: each clocked layer
  gets `round(total * clock)`. So the frame counts its clocks imply are exact only up to
  that rounding, and `scaled_to` can still raise if the target drops below the number of
  holds. It is the right tool — it is just not idempotent-by-accident, and the rounding is
  a place a two-frame drift can hide.

### 8.8 The frames decision — read this before you trust any document in this repo

The clearest case in the whole codebase of a *speed* measurement being allowed to overrule
a *look* judgement, and then being reverted. It is worth stating in full because a rebuild
will meet the identical temptation, and because **the documentation and the code
disagree**:

- **Measured:** 12 raw frames against 16, same prompt, same seed, same LoRA, one loaded
  pipeline. 12 was **27% faster** (510.0 s vs 695.4 s) with per-frame cost identical
  (41.8 vs 42.8 s) and alignment/coherence equal-or-better
  `[measured, recon_test/bench_frames.py]`.
- **Written down:** "`frames` is now a per-production setting defaulting to **12** for new
  productions" `[measured, HANDOFF.md §0.4]`.
- **Then reversed.** The default in code is **16** `[measured, film_store.py:134-152]`, and
  the reason is recorded in the source: `bench_frames` does **not** measure perceived
  motion; AnimateDiff's motion module was trained on 16-frame windows; and the owner's
  verdict on the 12-frame takes was *"the videos used to actually move, now it's still."*
  Tests and the studio now assert 16 as a correction
  `[measured, recon_test/test_film_store.py:391-392, ui/studio.py:1078-1080]`.

**The transferable rule, and it applies directly to a cel pipeline:** the number of
drawings is not primarily a cost parameter. It is the thing the audience perceives as
whether the picture lives. `cost_report()` is there to make the price *visible* before you
commit — not to talk you into a cheaper number. A rebuild will have its own version of
this temptation, and it will look just as reasonable: *"on threes is 33% fewer drawings
than on twos."* It is. Ask the eye anyway, and write the answer down where the code can
read it, because a stale `HANDOFF` will otherwise tell the next engineer the opposite.

## 9. NOTHING GETS TRAINED. This is a constraint, not a preference.

This chapter exists because the first draft of this document broke the rule in two places,
and the rule is not negotiable. It is the owner's standing position, recorded in this repo
from the beginning:

> **"training anything ('takes forever just to produce mediocre results')"** —
> `HANDOFF.md:276`, in the list of things the user does **not** want.

That is a requirement with the same status as "no slideshow" and "no GPU purchase." Any
plan that quietly needs a fine-tune has failed before it starts, and it will fail *slowly*,
which is worse.

### 9.1 The architecture needs zero training

Every learned component is **pretrained and used as shipped**:

| Component | Status | Trained by |
|---|---|---|
| line extraction | `Anime2Sketch` (MIT), or Simo-Serra 2016 sketch cleanup | someone else, years ago |
| flat fill | `BasicPBC` / inclusion matching (CVPR 2024), or Style2Paints | someone else |
| pose / rough conditioning | `ControlNet` (lineart, openpose) | someone else |
| board generation | any existing style checkpoint, **as shipped** | someone else |
| depth, if ever needed | `Depth Anything` | someone else |
| ink & paint | **deterministic code** — segmentation + indexed fill | nobody. It is a `for` loop. |
| multiplane camera | **deterministic code** — affine transforms | nobody |
| line boil, dry-brush, paper, grade | **deterministic code** — seeded procedural | nobody |

**There is no fine-tune, no LoRA, no DreamBooth, no distillation, and no custom checkpoint
anywhere on the critical path.** Two things this buys beyond the time saved:

- **The look is reviewable.** A boil parameter, a fill table and a curve can be read,
  diffed, and argued with. A fine-tune cannot — when it is wrong you have a mood, not a
  bug report.
- **It is the reason consistency is even reachable.** Consistent characters come from a
  **paint chart** and a **model-sheet anchor** (§4.1), both of which are *data*, not
  weights. A character LoRA would be the trained version of a job the chart already does
  deterministically — and would do it worse, because weights cannot be audited.

### 9.2 The two places the first draft violated it, and what replaces them

**Violation 1 — "board painting with a gouache/watercolor LoRA."** A style LoRA means
training on a corpus. There is no licensed 1950s Disney background corpus, so the corpus
would be Disney's own frames, which is both a rights problem and a training problem.
**Replacement, in order of preference:**

1. **An off-the-shelf checkpoint used as shipped** that already reads as gouache or
   watercolor on board. No training: pick it and use it.
2. **A procedural board.** The board is the *least* load-bearing component in the whole
   pipeline — it is a flat painting that gets panned past. A layered procedural board
   (washes, a paper grain, a few painted masses from a small palette) is honest, instant,
   fully reviewable, and it cannot drift to a different art style two hundred setups later.
3. If neither satisfies, **re-scope the shot** rather than reach for training.

**Violation 2 — the implied IP-Adapter / character-LoRA path to on-model consistency.**
§5.5 already argues against this on quality grounds (no published method takes a model
sheet as a hard constraint). It is now also ruled out on the training ground. The
replacement is what the document already specifies and is *stronger*: an `ANCHORED`
drawing rooted at the model sheet, plus a **closed paint chart** enforced on every cel.

### 9.3 What "no training" does not forbid

Be precise, because the rule is easy to over-apply and would strangle the system:

- **Running a pretrained model is not training.** Inference is fine, including running it
  on your own drawings.
- **Deterministic calibration is not training.** Tuning a boil amplitude, a fill table, a
  grade curve, or a threshold by eye is *authoring*. That is the work. It is fast, it is
  reversible, and it is exactly what this architecture is made of.
- **Caching is not training.** The hash-keyed cache pattern in §8.2 stays.
- **The existing "distillation" files in this repo are not part of this plan.** They belong
  to the superseded stack and are not a precedent.

**The test to apply to any proposed component: "does this require a gradient step?"**
If yes, it is out. If it is a table, a curve, a threshold or a pretrained forward pass, it
is in.

## 11. Traps to carry over, and traps that are new

### Carried over — these cost real renders here and will cost them again

1. **A check that fires on good work is the check being wrong.** The repo tried a
   "recurring subject must be present at both ends" story rule; it fired on 2 of 3
   constructed good cases and was deleted, with the reason recorded
   `[measured, HANDOFF §11.3]`. Expect the same for any on-model or style rule you add.
2. **Prove the acceptance test can fail.** The camera-resample test passed on three
   convenient shots and failed on two real ones `[measured, HANDOFF §6.1]`. Test every
   case, and pick a check the failure *cannot survive* — the sentinel fill colour, not
   "no black pixels".
3. **A function whose argument can be a record or an id must accept both.** The
   `next_take_name` bug threw away 3–5 minutes of loaded pipeline because it was handed a
   dict `[measured, HANDOFF §0.2 trap 1]`. Keep `film_store._sid`.
4. **Adding a key to a hash invalidates every existing take.** The costliest bug in the
   repo's history `[measured, HANDOFF §0.4 trap 9]`. Append to the tolerance list; never
   write a new default into an existing project.
5. **A test can measure the earlier tests instead of the code** — one test failed three
   times and every failure was the expectation `[measured, HANDOFF §0.2 trap 8]`. And a CSS
   test passed *wrongly* by matching an explanatory comment. Build fixtures per test.
6. **No metric has ever settled a quality question here.** `align` called 4-step and
   12-step identical; `global_share` and `loc10` disagreed in direction on the same pair
   `[measured, HANDOFF §11.1]`. Build the side-by-side and look. For this project that
   means a **cel A/B strip**, and it means eyes on the output at every look decision.
7. **A silent merge is a motion bug that looks like a saving.** `canonical_states()`
   merged two distinct apexes because the prompt bands were too coarse, and destroyed a
   bounce `[measured, ANIMATION_SPEC.md §6.1]`. In this pipeline the equivalent is a paint
   chart whose entries are too close to distinguish, or a level whose origin rounding
   collapses two poses into one drawing. **Report every merge.**
8. **Caches must live inside the writable tree.** A sandbox that denies `HF_HOME`,
   `TORCH_HOME` and `TEMP` outside the workspace will bite the new stack too.

### New — specific to a cel pipeline

9. **Do not let a model produce the line.** If the line is diffusion output it will have
   soft edges and the flat fill will look like a posterized photograph. Line must be a
   *mask*; paint must be an *index*.
10. **Anti-aliased fills kill the look.** A flat region with a 1px gradient boundary reads
    as vector art. Decide the edge policy explicitly (hard, or hard with a deliberate
    over-inked boundary) — and pick it before you have 200 cels to re-render.
11. **Perfect stability reads as dead.** No boil means digital. But *too much* boil reads
    as a wobbling JPEG. This is a tonal decision with a real parameter range and it needs
    eyes, not a metric.
12. **Boards must be painted bigger than the camera needs, and the required size must be
    computed from the authored camera move** — a board painted to frame size cannot be
    trucked into, and discovering this after the board is painted costs a repaint.
13. **The board is lit, the cel is flat.** Constantly conflating these is the most common
    way a synthetic "cel" image ends up looking like a painting with a sticker on it.
14. **`clock = 0` is not "no layer".** A static background is still a level with a plane
    and a board. The existing `Layer.is_static` collapses it to local frame 0
    `[measured, animation_spec.py:589]` — correct for exposure, but the *compositor* must
    still place it, and it must still move with the camera.
15. **The proposal cache has no run id, and it is used as if it were history.** The key is
    `{concept_slug}__{model}__n{N}` `[measured, story.py:406-412]`, so `--fresh` re-rolls of
    the same concept and model **overwrite** the previous proposal on disk. The audit
    records that this destroyed a whole round of samples — 12 leaked prompts lost
    `[measured, writer_audit.py:370-372]`. Two rules: add a run id (or a content hash) to
    the key, and treat a cache directory as a cache, never as a record. Anything you want
    to keep, write somewhere else with a timestamp.
16. **A closed set of colours is only closed if something checks it.** Criterion 6 of §12 is
    the guarantee the architecture buys, and it buys nothing if no test asserts it. Every
    cel that leaves the paint stage should be validated against its chart, and a *near*
    miss (a colour one step off) is the failure mode to watch — it will pass an
    eyeball check on one cel and read as dirt across two hundred.
17. **The documentation can be confidently wrong, and the code is the truth.** The clearest
    instance: `HANDOFF.md` states that new productions default to **12** frames, and the
    code defaults to **16** and says why `[measured, §8.8]`. Before you implement anything
    from a prose document in this repo — including this one — read the constant. Where the
    two disagree, the constant wins and the prose is a bug.
18. **A post-processing library needs three tests, and this repo already wrote them.**
    `recon_test/test_effects.py` asserts, for every registered effect:
    **`amount=0` is a bit-exact no-op**; **the same input and seed give the same output**
    (so a preview *is* the export); and **`amount=1.0` measurably changes the image**
    (a "control that does nothing" fails the suite) `[measured, §2, §3, §4 of that file]`.
    It also asserts that **every parameter set to a zero value actually reaches the
    function** — the bug there was filtering overrides by truthiness, which silently made
    `softness=0.0` and `ordered=False` unsettable `[measured, §6]`. Port all four rules to
    the ink/paint/boil parameters. A look library with no no-op guarantee cannot be
    composed, and one whose parameters cannot be set to zero cannot be tuned.
19. **A cosmetic stage must not be able to kill a render.** `apply_chain` catches an
    unknown effect or a failing step, **skips it, and keeps the frames** — *"the takes are
    the expensive thing and they are already on disk"* `[measured, effects.py:897-900]`.
    `validate_chain` reports the bad step as `"step N: no effect called 'x'"` rather than
    skipping silently. Both halves are needed: report the problem, do not destroy the work.
    This applies directly to the cel finisher — a boil or a paper texture that throws must
    cost you the effect, never the cels.

## 12. Acceptance criteria

The rebuild is done when all of the following are true and demonstrable:

1. `animation_spec.py`'s invariant tests pass **unmodified**, plus new tests for levels,
   planes and per-level clocks.
2. **Zero sentinel-fill pixels** across every delivered frame of every shot.
3. `cost_report()["generation_calls"]` equals the number of distinct *element* drawings,
   and a held cel costs **zero** generator calls. A frame count of N with D drawings makes
   D generator calls, not N.
4. Assembled frame count equals `round(duration × fps)` with no pad and no trim.
5. No code path constructs `DrawingSource.CHAINED`; no character drawing anchors to
   anything but the model sheet's `AUTHORED` root.
6. **Every colour in every delivered cel is in that character's or that scene's paint
   chart.** This is mechanically checkable and is the consistency guarantee the whole
   architecture buys.
7. Parallax is monotonic in plane depth; the action plane is exactly 1.0; the required
   board size is computed and reported before boards are painted.
8. A re-render with the same seeds, chart revisions and camera produces **byte-identical
   frames** — the composite is deterministic.
9. A human looks at a 20-second sequence and says it reads as a 1950 picture.
10. **Nothing in the delivered pipeline required a gradient step.** Every learned component
    is a pretrained forward pass; everything else is a table, a curve, a threshold or a
    transform (§9).

Criterion 9 is the only one that matters, criterion 6 is the one that makes it reachable,
and criterion 10 is the one that keeps the project finishable.

## 13. Research notes — what is sourced, and what I could not verify

The historical and tooling claims above were researched against public sources. This
section exists so the document is **auditable** rather than authoritative-sounding: where a
claim is an inference it says so, and where the research failed it says that too.

### 13.1 Verified

- **Pipeline order and job titles**: storyboards → story reel / leica reel → timing director
  → exposure sheet → layout → key animation (pencil test, sweatbox) → assistant animators →
  **cleanup department** (cleanup animators + inbetweeners) → ink & paint → background →
  camera. Character animation and **effects animation were separate departments**.
- **Ink & paint mechanics**: ink on the **front** of the cel, opaque paint on the
  **reverse**; the **~five-level stack ceiling** and why it forced opacity.
- **Xerography dates** — first tested in scenes of *Sleeping Beauty* (1959), first fully
  used in *Goliath II*, first feature entirely xerographic *One Hundred and One Dalmatians*
  (1961). **Therefore *Cinderella* (1950) was hand-inked and hand-painted.** The premise
  holds.
- **Line colour**: gray outlines on characters, white/blue toners for snow and water, and
  **"self-colored lines"** as the term of art.
- **Peg and field conventions**: Acme (two slots, one round hole) most common; Oxberry
  rostrum cameras with 2 peg bars (Master Series 4); 12F ≈ 12in × 8.85in; 16F the largest
  common; the drawing centre **5.75in above the round peg hole**, marked "Academy centre."
- **Exposure sheet layout**: phonetic dialogue column, **one column per level**, a camera
  column for pan/truck/field size, sequence/scene headers, and **on-ones / on-twos /
  on-threes** as the exposure convention.
- **Multiplane**: Garity, completed early 1937, first tested in *The Old Mill*; **6 layers
  per D23, 7 per Wikipedia** (sources differ — report both); **oils on glass**; the
  **moving distortion glass** ripple on water; planar lighting and filters in-camera;
  **pans move the artwork, not the camera**; a crew of up to a dozen.
- **Technicolor**: **three-strip Process 4** with **IB dye-transfer** printing; cameras
  rented, not bought, with a colour supervisor; the strips have different contrast and
  shrink at different rates, with **green as the alignment reference**; the 2023 4K
  restoration used the **original 35mm Technicolor negative from the Library of Congress**.
- **The ML tooling** in §5.5: `Anime2Sketch`, Simo-Serra 2016, `BasicPBC`/inclusion matching
  (CVPR 2024), `Style2Paints` (arXiv **1706.03319** — the commonly cited 1705.01908 is
  wrong), split filling (CVPR 2021), `AnimeInterp`, `EISAI` (**2111.12792** — 2206.02713 is
  *not* EISAI), `AnimeInbet`, `JoSTC`, `ToonCrafter`, `ToonComposer`, `RIFE`, `FILM`,
  `ControlNet`, `IP-Adapter`, `InstantID`, `Depth Anything`, and the survey
  *Generative AI for Cel-Animation* (**arXiv 2501.06250**, ICCV 2025 AISTORY workshop).
  The ToonCrafter quotations about drifting, dissolving, and latent degradation being worse
  on "high-contrast regions, the fine structural outline, and the lack of motion blur" are
  the paper's own words.
- **Line boil cadence**: the `hold` parameter in `scofano/comfy-line-boil` — same pattern
  across consecutive frames, new pattern between drawings.

**The honest summary of the research:** the *pipeline* is settled and the *tooling* is
identified. What is open is decorative — a numbered palette, a line-colour taxonomy, and
plane spacing. **None of the three is load-bearing.** §4.2's depth values are `[design]`
and can be tuned by eye; the paint chart is authored, not discovered. Do not hold up the
build for them.

## 14. Summary: what actually changed

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| unit of work | a 12–16 frame video clip | a drawing of one element on one level |
| frame | generated | **composited** |
| motion | from the model (global drift) | from the **exposure sheet** + the **camera** |
| consistency | hoped for from a prompt and a seed | **indexed from a paint chart**, anchored to a model sheet |
| background | regenerated per shot | **painted once per scene**, panned under the cels |
| depth | modelled | **geometry** (planes and parallax) |
| the model's job | make the picture | make a *drawing*; then get out of the way |
| determinism | none | everything downstream of the cel |
| where the risk is | the whole render | **line fidelity and flat paint** |
| what to build first | the renderer | the **compositor**, on coloured rectangles |

The through-line: **this repo already knows that the future should not be inferred from
the previous frame.** A cel pipeline is the purest possible expression of that idea — the
picture was never generated at all, it was drawn, painted, and photographed. You are not
building a video model. You are building a **camera department**.
